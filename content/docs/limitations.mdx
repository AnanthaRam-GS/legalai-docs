These are the known technical limits of LegalAI as it stands, by area.

## Coverage

- **A small corpus.** Answers draw on 70 reviewed judgments. Many questions will fall outside it; LegalAI is designed to say so rather than answer from general knowledge, but a "no coverage" answer means only that the corpus lacks material, not that no law applies.
- **Criminal law only,** in English, centred on the IPC, BNS, CrPC and BNSS and a few special Acts cited in judgments.
- **The IPC to BNS transition** is thinly covered: BNS and BNSS account for 33 of the 920 section citations extracted from the corpus, and only 3 of the 30 benchmark queries test the transition.

## Answers

- **Grounding is checked structurally at run time.** Citation validation confirms that each claim cites evidence that was actually retrieved, with matching metadata. It does not prove that the cited passage supports the claim: on an AI review of a 15-query sample with full passages, about two-thirds of citations were fully supported and about three-quarters at least partly ([Results](/results/#evaluation-2026-10)).
- **Transition-law errors.** Answers can misstate how the IPC and the BNS apply across 1 July 2024. Of the 7 citations the review still judged to state the wrong law, 3 were transition reasoning: that murder after 1 July 2024 is still IPC 302, that proof rather than the offence date decides between IPC 302 and BNS 103, and that both can apply to the same act.
- **Case names in the answer text are not validated.** Only structured citations are checked. In the October 2026 evaluation the full pipeline named 2 real cases in its answer text that were not in its retrieved evidence.
- **Section titles are checked; section names in prose are not.** Each section the answer lists is shown with a trusted title (official heading, catalog or mapping) or marked "Unverified label", and a wrong statute is corrected only when the label or the uploaded document clearly points to the sister statute. Section names inside sentences, such as the summary, are not resolved; the FIR brief on the [Demo](/demo/) page shows one. The flag that marks a model label differing from the trusted title (often only a synonym) is kept in the data and not shown.
- **CrPC is catalog-only.** No reliable official machine-readable CrPC exists (India Code holds only a 1974 scan), so CrPC sections have no official headings: 5 of the 105 CrPC sections cited in the corpus have a catalog title and the rest stay unverified. Overall, 214 of the 370 cited sections have a trusted title.
- **Relative relevance is not a probability.** It ranks precedents within one answer; a score of 1.0 means "best of these five", not "certainly relevant".
- **Predicted sections are signals,** used to guide retrieval, not legal conclusions.
- **OCR is English by default and can misread scans.** Other languages need extra Tesseract language data and a configuration change. Low-confidence pages are flagged, but names, dates and figures from scans should be checked against the original.

## Capacity

- **Free-tier answer capacity.** Answers come from Groq's free tier, which allows each model about 8,000 tokens a minute and 200,000 tokens in a rolling 24 hours. One answer uses roughly 6,000–7,000 tokens, so a day allows about **30 answers per model**, and professional answers use one model only (gpt-oss-120b): about **30 professional answers a day** in total.
- At busy moments answers wait in a queue (up to 90 seconds, or 45 seconds for a professional answer) and then return a "busy" message with a retry time; when the day's capacity is used up, LegalAI says when to try again.
- Because of this, the evaluation, user acceptance testing and the demo are scheduled on **separate days**. A paid tier would raise both limits without code changes.

## Analytics

- Cited sections and outcomes are extracted by rules and are **unreviewed** until a qualified reviewer checks them. Outcome fields are empty when the rules find nothing or find conflicting values.
- The IPC-to-BNS mapping is a comparative reference, not statutory text, and is **unreviewed**. Nine of its rows have descriptions that conflict with the official heading; they are flagged in the dashboard, not edited. Its 68 NCRB-sourced rows have no change type, because NCRB does not print one, and CYTRAIN rows that cover several IPC sections under one group label are not split.
- NCRB figures are 2024 registrations. They do not describe convictions, acquittals or trends across years.

## Evaluation

- Retrieval quality was measured on **30 queries** labelled by **one reviewer**, using pooled rather than exhaustive relevance, over the same small corpus. The figures are descriptive, not statistically significant; an expansion by about 70 queries is in progress.
- The October 2026 answer evaluation covers **30 queries** (24 answered by all three configurations). Whether citations support their claims was judged on a **15-query sample** by an **AI chat assistant** (not Claude Code), blind to configuration and **not verified by a person**: round 1 on 600-character excerpts, round 2 on full passages for the citations round 1 did not call fully supported, after seeing round 1's verdicts. B and C cannot be told apart on that sample.
- There has been **no user acceptance testing** yet.
- **The InLegalBERT party judge is AI-labelled.** The held-out set that decided the adoption of InLegalBERT for Parties & Roles (5 judgment excerpts, 17 parties) was labelled by an AI assistant and not verified by a person, its confidence intervals are wide, and the accused-role fix was not tested on real FIRs or charge sheets. See [Experiments](/experiments/).

## Engineering

- Per-IP and per-user rate limits, the generation cap and the answer-model queue live in memory, so they reset on restart and do not span several server processes. Only the daily model usage is saved to disk.
- Logout does not revoke a copied token before it expires (60 minutes).
- The system has not yet been deployed.
